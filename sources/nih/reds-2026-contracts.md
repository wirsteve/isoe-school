# NHLBI REDS program — September 2026 contract round (NIH RePORTER records)

Source: NIH RePORTER public API (https://api.reporter.nih.gov/v2/projects/search), query: fiscal
year 2026 projects whose titles contain "REDS" or "Recipient Epidemiology". Human-readable search:
https://reporter.nih.gov/ (search "Recipient Epidemiology and Donor Evaluation Study").
Retrieved: 2026-11-13. Tier: **federal award records** (facts about who holds which contract and
for what period) — not regulation, not guidance. Contract numbers beginning 75N980… are NHLBI
contracts (the "D" in the number denotes an indefinite-delivery contract vehicle — treat that
reading as unverified; don't teach it).

**What this establishes (and what it doesn't).** On 2026-09-30 NHLBI started a new round of REDS
contracts (base period 2026-09-30 to 2027-09-29): four regional hubs, a data coordinating center,
a center for transfusion laboratory studies, and REDS Brazil. The RePORTER abstracts for the hub,
DCC and CTLS contracts still describe the program as **REDS-IV-P** and say each contract "is for
phase 3" (dissemination, database closure, deliverables to NHLBI) — so these records do NOT show a
program formally named "REDS-V." The user refers to the current program as "RED V"/"REDS V";
lessons must say plainly what the records show and not assert a "REDS-V" name. Re-check NHLBI's
REDS page and RePORTER before teaching any later phase name.

### 75N98026D00095-0-759802600001-1 — REDS DATA COORDINATING CENTER
- Organization: WESTAT, INC.
- Project period (as listed): 2026-09-30 to 2033-09-29
- Abstract (verbatim):

> The objectives of the Recipient Epidemiology and Donor Evaluation Study-IV-Pediatric (REDS-IV-P) program is to evaluate and improve the safety and availability of the blood supply, as well as the safety and effectiveness of transfusion therapies, with attention to not only adults, but also neonates and children who need transfusion. REDS-IV-P is a seven-year multicenter program, in the US and in Brazil, charged with conducting epidemiologic and laboratory studies in blood donors and transfusion recipients. The REDS-IV-P Program is comprised of 4 domestic hubs and a Brazil transfusion program involving investigators from the U.S. and from the Brazilian blood centers. Each domestic hub has a blood center and transfusion services that serve several specialty care and community hospitals participating in REDS-IV-P (total of 22 hospitals). The domestic Hubs and the Brazil international programs are each supported by a Data Coordinating Center (DCC) and Center for Transfusion Laboratory Studies (CTLS). REDS-IV-P is divided into 3 phases: 1) Phase 1 (Years 1-2) includes development of the REDS-IV-P research protocols, the development and pilot of a linked donation-component-recipient (vein to vein) database, the conduct of phase 1 research studies, and the conduct of data analyses using existing transfusion databases; 2) Phase 2 (Years 3-5) includes the conduct of phase 2 research protocols, compilation of the vein to vein database and data analyses, and dissemination of findings through presentations and publications; and 3) Phase 3 (Years 6-7) includes dissemination of findings through presentations and publications, closure of databases, and provision of all deliverables to NHLBI. This contract is for the DCC phase 3 activities.

### 75N98026D00101-0-759802600001-1 — REDS BRAZIL BASE PERIOD SEPTEMBER 30,2026 - SEPTEMBER 29,2027
- Organization: VITALANT 
- Project period (as listed): 2026-09-30 to 2033-09-29
- Abstract (verbatim):

> The Recipient Epidemiology and Donor Evaluation Study (REDS) Brazil Transfusion Safety Research contract is a critical element of the overall REDS Program designed to evaluate and improve the safety and availability of the blood supply, and of transfused products across the life span in those who need transfusion. The program is poised since 2006 to conduct and evaluate an emerging infectious agent quickly for its prevalence and incidence among blood donors, transfusion-transmission potential, risk factors, and/or clinical relevance to the blood recipient population. The new iteration, with a pre-existing infrastructure already in place, will be accomplished over a seven-year multicenter program in Brazil. It will be charged with conducting epidemiologic and laboratory studies in blood donors and transfusion recipients. This cohesive and comprehensive multicenter program will use a multi-targeted strategy as part of the overall REDS effort to proactively address potential threats to the nation’s blood supply; evaluate how to enhance the effectiveness and safety of transfusions; and serve as a resource for ongoing work in transfusion research. The REDS Brazil program infrastructure will allow for evaluation of key research questions in transfusion medicine that are best studied through the analysis of a comprehensive set of epidemiological data on donors, donations, components, and transfused recipients compiled in core databases as well as through the conduct of stand-alone multi-site laboratory and observational studies. Like its predecessor programs, REDS Brazil will include a rapid research response capability to address emerging threats to the blood supply. The program will be poised to evaluate a new infectious agent quickly for its prevalence and incidence among blood donors, transfusion-transmission potential, risk factors, and clinical relevance to the blood recipient population and provide these data to inform the implementation of enhanced blood safety measures and policies. Results from this body of research continue to inform and impact the U.S. and global blood screening policies and strategies for preventing transmission of these infectious agents via blood transfusion.
