**IMPORTANT SCOPE CAVEAT for drafters:** this presentation is specifically about CDER's Good
Clinical Practice (GCP) inspections of clinical investigators and sponsors in drug trials — NOT
about CGMP inspections of a manufacturing facility, and NOT about blood establishments or BECS.
**Do not present this document as blood-establishment-specific or CGMP-specific guidance.** It is
used here only because FDA's post-inspection classification scheme — NAI/VAI/OAI — is a
general, FDA-wide practice (used across CDER, CBER, and ORA inspections of every kind, including
CGMP inspections of blood establishments), and this is the clearest FDA-authored, public,
verified source this curriculum has found explaining that scheme in FDA's own words. Cite the
classification scheme itself as general FDA inspection-outcome practice, verified via this
source; do not attribute GCP-specific statistics, case examples, or clinical-trial-specific
content to blood establishments.

## Slide 12 — "Final Inspection Outcome"

> NAI / VAI / OAI
>
> NAI: no violations identified
>
> VAI: violations identified but do not meet the threshold for OAI
>
> OAI: serious noncompliance, repeated or deliberate failure to comply with the regulations
>
> NAI: No Action Indicated
> VAI: Voluntary Action Indicated
> OAI: Official Action Indicated

## Slide 13 — "Why should we submit a 483 response?"

> Form FDA 483 → Your Written Response: Mitigate / Disprove / Clarify. May include supporting
> documentation, your acknowledgement, your commitment. [Response window:] 15 business days.

## Slide 14 — "What to consider in your 483 response?"

> Submit timely response. Include a commitment. Address each observation. Note: if agree or
> disagree. Provide corrective or preventive actions. Provide timeline. Provide method. Submit
> documentation.

## Slide 16 — "Official Action Indicated – OAI"

> Significant and serious, and/or numerous regulatory violations — Repeated, deliberate;
> Falsified or fabricated data submitted to sponsor or FDA. Scope, severity, or pattern of
> violations — Unreasonable and significant risk to subjects; Subjects' rights seriously
> compromised; Data integrity or reliability compromised.

(Note: "risk to subjects" and "subjects' rights" are GCP/clinical-trial-specific language —
don't transplant those exact phrases into a blood-establishment CGMP context; the *general
severity logic* — repeated/deliberate violations, data integrity compromised — transfers, the
specific examples don't.)

## Slide 17 — "OAI – Warning Letter (WL)"

> Available to the public (redacted). Informal and advisory. An opportunity to improve
> compliance. Follow-up inspection [may occur].

## Slide 19 — "Follow-up Inspection"

> To ensure violations are not repeated. To verify implementation of corrective/preventive
> actions. To ensure compliance is sustained.
